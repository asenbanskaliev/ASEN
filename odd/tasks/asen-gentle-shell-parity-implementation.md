## Execution guide: first, in parallel, then

This is a reading and execution-order guide, not another task list or permission to code.
P00–P44 are stable labels: keep the existing Tasks and card contracts as the authority.
Waves below mean priority queues, not dates or barriers between whole stages.
Take any ready, independent cut when its own prerequisites are ready; do not wait for a row to finish.
Repeated labels mean continuation of the same work, not duplicate tasks.

**First: bind identity, then discover in three read-only lanes.**
Start with the minimum P00 identity needed to bind the scoped comparison.
Then use lane A for P01 public entries; lane B for P02 instruction consumers and relevant P19 findings;
lane C for P03 host/admission findings, then relevant P04/P36/P40 feasibility.
P01/P02/P03 findings gate only their relevant consumers, not completion of every discovery lane.
P24 likewise supplies the specific permission findings a consumer needs, not all modes first.
Keep discovery findings, implementation changes and acceptance evidence separate.

**In parallel: choose up to three ready slots, not three compulsory writers.**
The compact queue covers every existing label; each cell is a possible work slot.
Arrows mean relevant discovery findings unless the card explicitly types another dependency.
They do not add mandatory implementation edges or require patches where no gap exists.

and measured need. P42 waits for the relevant individual acceptances and journey permissions.

**Then: follow the relevant findings, not a chain of compulsory patches.**

and actual native authority. A source-ready candidate never supplies that authority.
All future code still needs expanded human implementation scope, each card's FA02 admission
and an observed delta. Discovery readiness alone is not permission to develop.

**Keep parallel work safe and small.**
One parent integrates; ALL `extensions/asen.ts` edits are serialized, even different handlers.
Keep single owners for profiles/fallback/session, agent/runner/journals, Todo/dispatch and
review/candidates contracts. Stabilize shared contracts before consumer writers start;
separate worktrees do not remove semantic conflicts or allow competing stores/owners.
Cap the team at three agents: typically two independent writers plus a high-risk verifier
when called for, not an automatic verifier for every small task.
Use writers only for two or more truly independent behavior units heavy enough to benefit;
otherwise work inline. Keep tests and docs with the same behavior owner.
Before each future writer, derive exact allowed paths from its card; no generic root globs.
Do not duplicate existing Stop controls or keymap startup registration.

**Existing stops and evidence do not change.**
EQ09–EQ14 freezes and no-retry rules remain unchanged; fresh investigation cannot substitute
for stopped proof. Historical F10 audit/hash/readiness receipts describe older snapshots,
not an audit of this guide. Do not relabel them or edit frozen reports/matrices.
This guide is documentary only: no renewed parity, native approval or global readiness claim.
